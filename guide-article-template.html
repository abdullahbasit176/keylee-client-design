Snagging and handover

What to check before you accept a new unit

By [Author name] · Updated [date] · [X] min read

[Featured photo · Hands at work: a snagging checklist on a clipboard in a new apartment]
2400 × 960 px · WebP
The short answer

Inspect the unit before you sign the handover documents. List every defect with a photo, send the list to the developer in writing, and check each fix before you accept the keys.

When to inspect

As soon as the developer gives you access, and before you sign anything that confirms you’ve accepted the unit. Defects found after the handover is signed tend to take longer to get fixed.

What to check, room by room

  • Walls and ceilings. Cracks, damp patches, uneven paint.
  • Floors and tiles. Hollow or cracked tiles, gaps, scratches.
  • Doors and windows. Alignment, locks, seals and handles.
  • Plumbing. Water pressure, leaks under sinks, drainage, hot water.
  • Electrics. Every socket, switch and light.
  • AC. Cooling in every room, noise, drainage.
  • Kitchen and bathrooms. Fittings, sealant and cabinets.

Take a photo of every snag, with something in the frame for scale. A list without photos is easy to dispute.

What to do if you find defects

  1. Write each snag down with its room and a photo.
  2. Send the list to the developer in writing, and keep the reply.
  3. Agree a date for the fixes.
  4. Inspect again before you accept the keys.

If you can’t be there

A power of attorney lets someone inspect and accept the unit on your behalf. See POA services for how that works.
